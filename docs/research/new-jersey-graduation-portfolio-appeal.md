# New Jersey Graduation Portfolio Appeal: Versioned Workflow Case Study

- **Research date:** 2026-08-04
- **Primary profile:** New Jersey, Graduation Portfolio Appeal, 2025-2026 school year / Class of 2026
- **Future-cohort note:** Limited confirmed information for 2026-2027 / Class of 2027 is recorded separately
- **Status:** Foundation research input; not legal advice, compliance certification, or an operative submission profile

## 1. Purpose and scope

This document studies the New Jersey Graduation Portfolio Appeal as a concrete example of a regulated portfolio workflow. It is intended to expose the identity, eligibility, evidence, scoring, review, submission, retention, accommodation, and external-decision concepts that a future Vitrine compliance profile may need.

The findings are deliberately versioned. They must not be treated as permanent New Jersey rules, generalized to another jurisdiction, or embedded as universal Vitrine behavior. Before any operational use, the responsible district must revalidate the governing regulation, current NJDOE guidance, restricted NJDOE Homeroom templates, applicable records schedules, and local policy.

Vitrine may eventually help an authorized school team gather evidence, evaluate profile completeness, preserve provenance, and issue a reviewable snapshot. It must not:

- determine that a student is legally eligible without authorized source records and human review;
- create or score locally designed Constructed Response Tasks (CRTs) as an assessment authority;
- sign a Statement of Assurance;
- submit to NJDOE Homeroom unless a separately authorized integration is created;
- approve or deny a portfolio appeal;
- award a diploma;
- certify that a district complied with law or policy; or
- replace the district's official student record, submission receipt, or NJDOE outcome letter.

Sources are identified by the IDs in [source-register.md](source-register.md).

## 4. Graduation-assessment pathways and portfolio position

For the Class of 2026, NJDOE presents three ways to satisfy the graduation assessment requirement:

1. meet the required score on the applicable NJGPA component;
2. meet the requirement through an approved substitute competency assessment; or
3. demonstrate proficiency through the Graduation Portfolio Appeal. [NJ-REQ-01] [NJ-REG-01]

ELA and mathematics are separate components. A student may satisfy the two content areas through different pathways. The public FAQ states that attempting the second pathway is not a prerequisite to using the portfolio pathway. [NJ-FAQ-01]

The regulation places the portfolio appeals process after the student has taken the State graduation proficiency test, has not demonstrated proficiency in the relevant component, and has been provided remediation and one or more additional opportunities to demonstrate proficiency on the State test. The public Class of 2026 guides describe eligible participants as grade 12 students in the Class of 2026 who took the corresponding NJGPA component but had not yet met that component's graduation assessment requirement. [NJ-REG-01] [NJ-ELA-01] [NJ-MATH-01]

### Design implication

Eligibility cannot be represented by a single boolean copied from the presence of a portfolio. A future profile needs versioned eligibility predicates, evidence references supporting each predicate, the content area to which each predicate applies, human verification, and an explicit unknown state when required records are unavailable.

## 5. Four-layer workflow distinction

The New Jersey case requires strict separation among four layers.

### 5.1 Local working file

The school or district creates, administers, scores, and retains the substantive evidence. Depending on content area and variant, this includes:

- Education Proficiency Plan (EPP);
- current transcript;
- NJGPA and substitute-assessment performance documentation;
- intervention plan;
- qualifying ASVAB-AFQT documentation for the streamlined option;
- locally designed CRTs;
- passages, prompts, questions, rubrics, student responses, and scores;
- content-specific cover sheets;
- translations, transcripts, videos, and original responses where required;
- accommodation records needed to document correct administration; and
- local review and approval records.

NJDOE states that completed portfolio materials are stored onsite according to local retention policy and may be reviewed onsite at NJDOE's discretion. [NJ-APP-02]

### 5.2 External submission package and data

The public submission process identifies two principal files transmitted through the Portfolio Appeals project in NJDOE Homeroom for each school:

- a signed Statement of Assurance (SOA); and
- a Portfolio Appeals Data Spreadsheet containing student and content-area data, including CRT scores. [NJ-APP-02]

The complete evidence file is not described as the routine electronic upload. Public guidance instead requires the underlying materials to remain onsite. The restricted spreadsheet and field definitions control the exact transmitted data contract. [NJ-RST-01] [NJ-RST-02]

### 5.3 External decision

NJDOE reviews the submitted materials/data, may inspect onsite evidence, and provides outcome letters through the designated Portfolio Appeals Coordinator. The external outcome is not a Vitrine validation result. [NJ-APP-02]

### 5.4 Vitrine representation

A future Vitrine profile may represent:

- profile identity and source versions;
- content-area eligibility findings and supporting references;
- required local evidence and its availability;
- score and completeness findings;
- responsible actors and review status;
- restricted requirements that remain unverified;
- an immutable local review snapshot;
- an exact copy or reference to the externally submitted files, when authorized;
- a submission event and receipt reference; and
- a recorded external outcome and outcome-document digest.

It must keep each layer distinguishable in the data model and user interface.

## 7. Standard ELA appeal workflow

### 7.1 Eligibility

The public Class of 2026 ELA guide applies to a grade 12 Class of 2026 student who took the ELA component of NJGPA but had not yet met the ELA graduation assessment requirement. [NJ-ELA-01]

### 7.2 Required task composition

The standard ELA portfolio requires evidence from:

- two high-school-level reading passages, one literary and one informational, with associated comprehension items; and
- writing that includes at least two of the three NJSLS writing types: informative/explanatory, argument, and narrative. [NJ-ELA-01]

The guide describes a literary passage set and an informational passage set, each with comprehension items and a connected extended writing response. Items must align to approved ELA evidence statements, and writing is scored using the applicable NJSLA-ELA Grades 6-11 rubric. [NJ-ELA-01]

### 7.3 Locally controlled design decisions

The LEA or APSSD determines the number of comprehension items and total points. Passages should be high-school level; the guide suggests the grade 9-11 passage-length band. The local team may reuse CRTs from prior years only when they continue to meet current requirements. [NJ-ELA-01]

Released and practice materials may inform local task design, but actual NJSLA/NJGPA released or practice items and old AHSA materials may not be used as appeal evidence. Graded classwork is not a substitute for the specified CRT process. [NJ-ELA-01]

### 7.4 Passing requirements

For the standard ELA profile:

- the student must earn at least 50 percent on each reading task; and
- each writing task must earn at least 2 on each applicable rubric construct, with no half points. [NJ-ELA-01]

A future profile should represent the raw task score, possible points, rubric-construct scores, rubric identity/version, scorer, scoring time, and pass finding. It should not reduce the evidence to only a final pass flag.

### 7.5 Local ELA evidence inventory

The public guide identifies the following local file contents:

- completed EPP;
- current transcript;
- NJGPA and/or substitute competency assessment performance documentation;
- intervention plan;
- ELA CRTs;
- four graded responses: two reading and two writing; and
- completed ELA Portfolio Appeal Cover Sheet information for each graded response. [NJ-ELA-01]

## 8. Standard mathematics appeal workflow

### 8.1 Eligibility

The public Class of 2026 mathematics guide applies to a grade 12 Class of 2026 student who took the mathematics component of NJGPA but had not yet met the mathematics graduation assessment requirement. [NJ-MATH-01]

### 8.2 Required task composition

The standard mathematics portfolio requires four locally designed, administered, and scored CRTs:

- two distinct Type II/Subclaim C reasoning evidence statements;
- two distinct Type III/Subclaim D modeling evidence statements; and
- four distinct conceptual categories selected from Number and Quantity, Algebra, Functions, Geometry, and Statistics and Probability. [NJ-MATH-01]

The evidence statements may come from high-school NJSLA or NJGPA mathematics evidence-statement tables. Each CRT must be aligned to the selected evidence statement and preserve its conceptual category and task type.

### 8.3 Required CRT contents

The public guide describes each mathematics CRT package as including:

- a cover sheet;
- student identity;
- evidence-statement code and wording;
- content scope;
- conceptual category;
- reasoning or modeling classification;
- student score;
- the task;
- the student response; and
- the scoring rubric. [NJ-MATH-01]

### 8.4 Scoring constraints

The guide specifies total-point patterns for reasoning and modeling tasks, requires at least half of the available points to assess reasoning or modeling rather than computation, prohibits half-point awards, and permits submission only for tasks on which the student earned at least 50 percent of the available points. [NJ-MATH-01]

The profile should retain total points, earned points, reasoning/modeling points, computation points if present, rubric identity, score finding, and evidence-statement identity. A generic “math artifact” cannot establish compliance.

### 8.5 Task-source restriction

Released items may be used as examples for local design but may not be used as the actual appeal tasks. The profile therefore needs a task-origin attestation or review finding rather than copying secure/released assessment content into Vitrine by default. [NJ-MATH-01]

### 8.6 Local mathematics evidence inventory

The complete public guide should be rechecked when operationalizing the profile, but the evidence family includes:

- completed EPP and supporting records;
- four qualifying mathematics CRT packages for the standard variant;
- completed mathematics cover sheets;
- student responses and scores;
- local rubrics; and
- translations/originals when applicable. [NJ-MATH-01]

## 9. Streamlined variant

The Class of 2026 streamlined option applies when the student meets or exceeds an ASVAB-AFQT score of 35. It reduces the required quantity of CRTs by 50 percent. The qualifying score must be retained, and the EPP must identify use of the streamlined option. [NJ-STR-01]

| Content area | Streamlined evidence quantity |
| --- | --- |
| ELA | One successful reading task, either literary or informational, and one successful writing task connected to that reading |
| Mathematics | One successful reasoning task and one successful modeling task, using different conceptual categories |

The streamlined variant is not merely a display preference. It changes profile requirements and must be represented as an explicit, evidence-supported variant selection. Failure to retain the qualifying score or document the option should produce an incomplete/unknown finding, not automatic fallback to the standard variant.

## 10. Education Proficiency Plan and supporting records

The public content guidance identifies the EPP as a central local record. It includes or is supported by:

- the current transcript;
- performance on NJGPA and/or substitute competency assessments;
- an intervention plan implemented to help the student meet the graduation assessment requirement; and
- for streamlined appeals, documentation of the qualifying ASVAB-AFQT score and use of the streamlined option. [NJ-ELA-01] [NJ-MATH-01] [NJ-STR-01]

A future Vitrine profile should distinguish:

- required fact or finding;
- source record proving the fact;
- copied document versus external reference;
- sensitive data classification;
- local reviewer and review date;
- profile requirement version; and
- omission/redaction in any audience-specific export.

The EPP should not be flattened into a free-text note or treated as publicly shareable portfolio content.

## 12. Submission process, dates, and naming

For 2025-2026, NJDOE opened electronic submission on January 6, 2026. The preferred deadline for sufficient processing time before June graduation was May 1, 2026. Uploads could continue through August 26, 2026 for students expected to graduate with the Class of 2026. [NJ-APP-02] [NJ-SCHED-25]

The Portfolio Appeals Coordinator uses NJDOE Homeroom to obtain templates, upload files, view receipts, and retrieve outcome letters. A separate SOA and data spreadsheet are prepared for each school. The SOA requires signatures from the coordinator, high school principal, and chief school administrator. [NJ-APP-02]

The public guidance specifies year-, document-, CDS-code-, upload-date-based file names. The dates on the paired SOA and spreadsheet should match. Corrected files add `RESUBMISSION` to the file name. Multiple submissions are allowed during the window, and later-completing students may be added in subsequent submissions. [NJ-APP-02] [NJ-FAQ-01]

### Design implication

A future profile needs separate concepts for:

- profile deadline and preferred target date;
- subject eligibility period;
- local completion date;
- external submission batch;
- paired files within a batch;
- school/accountable identifiers;
- upload attempt and receipt;
- correction versus new-student incremental submission;
- superseded submission artifacts; and
- external outcome.

A working portfolio's issue date is not automatically the external submission date.

## 13. Special populations, accommodations, and language access

Public NJDOE guidance identifies several profile-specific requirements. [NJ-SP-01]

### 13.1 Students with disabilities and Section 504 plans

Required accommodations documented in an IEP or Section 504 plan must be implemented in the portfolio process. Accommodation details should remain purpose-limited and should not be exposed in general showcase or family editions merely because they were needed for administration.

Students participating in the Dynamic Learning Maps alternate assessment are not eligible to use the portfolio appeal in the same manner described by this profile. The exact relationship to alternate diploma requirements must remain governed by the student's IEP and current law/guidance. [NJ-SP-01] [NJ-REG-01]

### 13.2 American Sign Language

The guidance permits ASL responses with appropriate interpretation, video, and transcription treatment. A future profile must preserve the original response modality, exact media artifact, transcript relationship, interpreter/transcriber provenance where policy requires it, and accessible playback or alternate representation. [NJ-SP-01]

### 13.3 Multilingual learners and native-language responses

The ELA and mathematics guides permit a locally translated process:

1. prepare the CRT in English;
2. translate it into the student's native language using a locally selected process;
3. obtain the student's response in that language;
4. translate the response into English; and
5. retain both the original student response and English translation. [NJ-ELA-01] [NJ-MATH-01]

The regulation also recognizes native-language portfolio participation for multilingual learners when available, together with the applicable English language proficiency requirement. [NJ-REG-01]

The two artifacts must be linked without treating the translation as the original. Provenance should identify language, role, translation/transcription process, and exact source digests.

### 13.4 Students experiencing homelessness

The public guidance calls for coordination among relevant staff so that mobility or record-location issues do not prevent access to the process. Vitrine may help surface missing-source and staff-transition findings, but it cannot infer cross-district student identity or bypass record-transfer rules. [NJ-SP-01]

## 14. Review, attestation, correction, and outcome

### 14.1 Local review

Before submission, authorized staff should verify at least:

- content-area eligibility;
- correct standard or streamlined variant;
- EPP and supporting evidence;
- task quantity and category coverage;
- approved evidence-statement alignment;
- scoring/rubric requirements;
- required cover sheets;
- accommodation/translation handling;
- local evidence availability;
- spreadsheet data accuracy;
- SOA completion and signatures; and
- consistency between paired files and school identifiers.

A Vitrine completeness report may support this review but cannot replace the authorized reviewers' judgment or attestation.

### 14.2 Attestation

The public process says the SOA verifies integrity and compliance and requires three institutional signatures. Exact attestation wording is restricted. Vitrine should therefore represent the SOA as an external, versioned document requirement and never synthesize signature or attestation status from checked boxes alone. [NJ-APP-02] [NJ-RST-01]

### 14.3 Corrections and resubmissions

Corrected submissions use the required naming convention and `RESUBMISSION` marker. A corrected batch must not overwrite the prior submitted bytes or receipt in the audit history. It should supersede the prior submission for operational purposes while preserving both. [NJ-FAQ-01]

### 14.4 Outcome

The coordinator retrieves outcome letters and distributes them to authorized institutional leaders. Vitrine may record a structured outcome only when it is grounded in the authoritative letter or system result. The record should identify issuer, result, content area, subject, decision date, source document, digest, and recording actor. [NJ-APP-02]

## 15. Retention and audit considerations

NJDOE expects the substantive portfolio materials to be stored onsite under local retention policy and reserves the possibility of onsite review. [NJ-APP-02]

New Jersey regulation separately requires an accurate record of each student's Statewide assessment performance to be maintained for 100 years and requires transcripts to include applicable State-assessment results. The statewide student-record schedule distinguishes mandated student records, retained for 100 years, from permitted student records, retained for seven years after graduation. [NJ-REG-01] [NJ-RET-02]

These authorities do **not** establish that every Vitrine working file, copied artifact, draft, annotation, or generated snapshot belongs to the same record series. Classification must be made by the responsible district records authority using current schedules, Artemis, local policy, and the actual record's function. [NJ-RET-01] [LOCAL-01]

A future profile therefore needs:

- record-classification status: assigned, proposed, unknown, or not a record;
- authority and schedule reference;
- retention trigger and minimum period;
- legal hold or audit hold;
- authoritative record custodian;
- disposition approval and event;
- immutable submission and outcome history; and
- staff-transition/retrieval metadata.

## 16. Restricted and unresolved details

The following cannot be considered fully specified from public sources:

1. exact SOA text and all attestations;
2. exact spreadsheet columns, value domains, conditional requirements, and validation rules;
3. Homeroom permissions, upload error behavior, receipt schema, and outcome retrieval details;
4. local CRT approval and moderation procedures;
5. district records classification for every Vitrine object;
6. local translation, transcription, accommodation, and reviewer qualification procedures;
7. local identity and accountable-school mapping rules;
8. complete Class of 2027 portfolio requirements; and
9. any later corrections or policy memoranda not present in the public guidance family.

An operational profile should include an activation gate requiring an authorized reviewer to attach or validate the restricted artifacts and resolve locally controlled decisions.

## 19. Conclusion

The New Jersey Graduation Portfolio Appeal demonstrates that a regulated portfolio is not simply a collection of student work. It is a versioned institutional workflow combining content-area eligibility, locally created and scored evidence, intervention and supporting records, accommodations, formal attestation, restricted submission contracts, external review, outcome records, and records-management obligations.

The durable Vitrine lesson is not to encode New Jersey rules universally. It is to provide a profile-driven architecture that can preserve exact authority, evidence, provenance, human responsibility, uncertainty, immutable issuance, and external-system boundaries.
